Why you forget what you read

And the small habit that makes the good parts stick.

8 min read · Essays on attention

You finish a long article feeling sharper. It was clear, it was convincing, and you agreed with most of it. Ask yourself about it next Tuesday and you will find a pleasant fog where the argument used to be.

Most of what we read is gone within a week, and the loss is steepest in the first day.

This is not a character flaw. Memory is built to discard. Anything that is not used again is treated as noise, and an article read once, in one sitting, is almost never used again.

Reading is not the same as keeping

The readers who seem to remember everything are rarely the fastest. They are the ones who leave a trail: a highlight here, a sentence in their own words there, something they can come back to.

A note in your own words is a small act of rebuilding the idea. That rebuilding is exactly the work memory needs.

Each return trip is cheap, because you are not re-reading the whole piece. You are re-reading the two or three lines you decided mattered.

Highlights are a conversation with your future self. Keep them short enough that you will actually read them.
